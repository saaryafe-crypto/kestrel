# Evidence Audit for IG Content Rulebook
Compiled 2026-08-04. Only measured data, platform-official statements, and published studies. Evidence strength graded: STRONG (large-N peer-reviewed or platform-official), MEDIUM (large-N industry benchmark, methodology partially disclosed), WEAK (small-N or secondhand).

---

## 1. Format performance: carousels vs single images vs reels

**Socialinsider 2026 benchmark** — 35 million IG posts from 447,613 profiles, Jan–Dec 2025 data (MEDIUM: large-N industry data, non-peer-reviewed):
- Engagement rate by format (2025 full-year): carousels 0.55%, Reels 0.52%, single images 0.37%. Q2 2026 quarterly: carousels 0.50%, Reels 0.48%, images 0.33%.
- Platform-wide average engagement 0.48%, down 24% YoY. Single-image engagement and posting volume down ~17% YoY.
- Reels win reach: ~30.8% average reach rate (best discovery format). Carousels win saves: roughly 2x the saves of Reels per impression at larger account sizes (e.g., 50–100K followers: 35 saves/post carousel vs 22 reel vs 10 image).
- Views by size (100K–1M followers): carousels 35,370 vs Reels 16,035 vs images 22,900 per post.

**Rival IQ 2025 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report** — 4M+ posts, 9B interactions, 14 industries (MEDIUM):
- All-industry median IG engagement rate (by followers): 0.36%; top 25% of brands: 1.05%. Carousels top-performing or tied with Reels in most industries; Reels generate 2–3x the engagement of static images in most industries.

**Carousel slide count / swipe-through** — Socialinsider carousel study, 22M+ posts (2020, older data; MEDIUM):
- Average ER: carousels 1.92% vs images 1.74% vs videos 1.45% (note: pre-Reels-era absolute numbers, use only the ranking).
- Carousels using all 10 slides: 2.07% ER (only 6% of carousels used 10 slides). "Swipe left" CTA raised ER from 1.83% to 2.00%. Mixed video+image carousels: 2.33% ER.
- Carousel "double exposure" (unswiped carousels re-served in feed with the next slide) is stated by Instagram/trade press but NO published number quantifies the reach boost. NO DATA FOUND on measured swipe-through-rate → reach causality.

## 2. Emotional drivers of sharing

**Berger & Milkman 2012, "What Makes Online Content Viral?", Journal of Marketing Research** — all NYT articles over ~3 months (~7,000 articles; 20% made most-emailed list) (STRONG). Exact effects, % change in fitted probability of making the most-emailed list per +1 SD (verified from the paper's Figure 2 / Table 4):
- Anger +34% (equivalent to 2.9 extra hours as lead story on NYT homepage)
- Awe +30%; Practical value +30%; Interest +25%; Anxiety +21%; Emotionality +18%; Surprise +14%; Positivity +13%
- Sadness −16% (only tested emotion that reduced virality)
- Interpretation: high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) drive sharing; low-arousal (sadness) suppresses it. Positive content overall slightly more viral than negative, but arousal matters more than valence. Lab studies (2a/2b) confirmed causality.

**Tellis, MacInnis, Tirunillai & Zhang 2019, Journal of Marketing (video/ads replication)** — two field studies, 11 emotions, 60+ ad characteristics on YouTube video ads (STRONG):
- Amusement, excitement, inspiration, warmth positively drive sharing; information-focused content has a significantly NEGATIVE effect on sharing (except risky/expensive categories). Optimal length 1–1.5 min; early brand placement hurts shares. Drama elements (surprise, plot, characters incl. babies, animals, celebrities) arouse the emotions that drive sharing.

**Rathje, Van Bavel & van der Linden 2021, PNAS** — n = 2,730,215 posts from news media + US congressional accounts on Facebook/Twitter (STRONG):
- Each word referring to the political out-group increased odds of a share/retweet by 67%. Out-group animosity effect ~4.8x stronger than negative-affect language and ~6.7x stronger than moral-emotional language. Posts about the out-group shared ~2x as often as in-group posts.

No Instagram-specific peer-reviewed replication of Berger & Milkman found.

## 3. Negativity bias in news engagement

**Robertson, Pröllochs, Schwarzenegger, Pärnamets, Van Bavel & Feuerriegel 2023, "Negativity drives online news consumption," Nature Human Behaviour 7:812–822** — Upworthy Research Archive: ~105,000 headline variations, N = 22,743 RCTs, ~5.7M clicks, 370M+ impressions (STRONG, randomized):
- For a headline of average length, each additional NEGATIVE word increased CTR by 2.3% (relative). Positive words DECREASED CTR (~1.0% per word).
- By emotion: sadness-related words increased clicks; anger and fear words were less effective than sadness (per Nieman Lab's coverage of the paper); joy decreased clicks. Negativity attracts CLICKS — note this measures clicking, not sharing (Berger & Milkman measure sharing, where positivity/awe also work).

## 4. Headline/hook length and style

**Banerjee & Urminsky 2024/2025, "The Language That Drives Engagement," Marketing Science 44(3):566–592** — every Upworthy A/B test Jan 2013–Apr 2015: 32,487 experiments, 150,817 arms, 538,272,878 participant assignments (STRONG, randomized):
- 21 of 36 headline constructs significantly moved CTR; average CTR 1.6%; most effects within ±3.1% relative CTR per 1 SD.
- Confirmed positives: curiosity-inciting language (e.g., unresolved pronouns / forward reference) INCREASED CTR; NUMBERS in headlines INCREASED CTR; concreteness improved engagement; emotionally intense language increased engagement; social stories about individuals ("social singular": ~+6.4% CTR per SD) increased CTR.
- Contrarian findings: headlines with longer sentences, harder readability, and HIGHER word count were clicked MORE (on Upworthy); more positive-emotion words did not reliably help; present-focused (vs past) language reduced CTR.
- Big caveat from authors: prior industry advice often predicts the wrong DIRECTION of effects.

**"When curiosity gaps backfire," Scientific Reports 2024** — meta-analysis of 8,977 Upworthy headline experiments (STRONG):
- Inverted-U: when baseline headlines are vague, more concreteness INCREASES CTR; when already concrete, more concreteness DECREASES CTR. Neither pure curiosity-gap nor full-information wins; headlines conveying "just the right amount" of information maximize CTR. Explains why clickbait studies conflict.
- NO DATA FOUND for a clean "optimal headline word count" number from randomized data; length effects are confounded (Robertson controlled for length; Banerjee found longer clicked more on Upworthy; Science Advances found simpler preferred at WaPo — see item 7).

## 5. Faces in images

**Bakhshi, Shamma & Gilbert, CHI 2014, "Faces Engage Us"** — 1.1 million Instagram photos (STRONG):
- Photos with faces: 38% more likely to receive likes, 32% more likely to receive comments. Number of faces, age, and gender of faces made NO difference. Side finding: posting too much decreases likes ~2x faster than comments.

**Li & Xie 2020, Journal of Marketing Research, "Is a Picture Worth a Thousand Words?"** (STRONG, but nuance):
- Human face presence increased sharing on Twitter by more than 80% — but the face effect was NOT significant on Instagram in their brand-content sample. High-quality/professional images consistently lifted engagement on both platforms. Takeaway: the faces effect is robust for likes/comments (Bakhshi) but not guaranteed for every content type/platform metric.

## 6. Instagram algorithm — platform-official statements (Mosseri / IG official)

(All PLATFORM-OFFICIAL, as reported by trade press; Instagram does not publish effect sizes.)
- Jan 2025, Mosseri: the three most important ranking signals are (1) watch time, (2) likes per reach, (3) sends per reach. Likes per reach matters more for reaching followers; sends per reach matters more for unconnected/discovery reach. 2025–2026 messaging has emphasized SHARES/sends above all other signals.
- 2024 "sends per reach" doctrine: Mosseri explicitly advised making content people want to send to friends, while warning against tacky "share this" baiting.
- Apr–May 2024 official ranking overhaul (Instagram blog): (a) small/emerging creators get equal chance — eligible content is first shown to a small test audience of likely-interested non-followers, and expands with engagement; (b) originality: if two identical pieces of content exist, only the ORIGINAL is recommended, the original replaces the repost in Explore/Reels/feed recommendations; (c) serial aggregators (accounts reposting others' content they didn't materially enhance 10+ times in 30 days) are removed from recommendations entirely; reposts get labels linking the original creator.
- Meta-reported user behavior: stay-or-scroll decision on a reel in ~1.7 seconds (reported from internal data; secondhand).
- Implication for a news repost page: reposted/aggregated media without material transformation is explicitly demoted — this is the single most platform-official risk to the account model.

## 7. Reading level / language simplicity

**Rogers et al. 2024 (with Todd Rogers, HKS), "Reading dies in complexity," Science Advances 10(23)** — 30,000+ field experiments: Washington Post (8,972 experiments, 24,044 headlines, Mar 2021–Dec 2022) + Upworthy archive (STRONG, randomized):
- Readers systematically prefer simpler headlines (more common words, better readability); mechanism study: general public pays more attention to and processes simpler headlines more deeply, skipping complex ones. Professional journalists did NOT show this pattern (writers misjudge readers). Exact per-grade-level CTR lift not stated in abstract.
- Tension: Banerjee & Urminsky found harder-to-read Upworthy headlines clicked more; the WaPo dataset (mainstream news, closer to a news IG page) supports simpler. Net: for news audiences, simple wins.

**Pancer et al. 2019, Journal of Consumer Psychology, "How Readability Shapes Social Media Engagement"** — 4,000+ Humans of New York Facebook posts over 3 years (STRONG for likes/comments/shares as the metric):
- Easier-to-read posts got more likes, comments, and shares, controlling for photo features and story valence. HONY average readability grade 3.6 (SD 1.9). Long posts (>70 words) out-performed short (≤20 words) only when written at Grade 5/6 readability. Mechanism: processing fluency.

## 8. Posting frequency

**Buffer analysis (reported 2025)** — 2M+ posts from 100,000+ accounts (MEDIUM: large-N, methodology partially disclosed, correlational — high-output accounts may differ in quality/resources):
- vs posting 1–2x/week: 3–5 posts/week → >2x follower growth rate; 6–9/week → up to 3.7x; 10+/week → up to 5.5x. Consistent posting → ~5x engagement vs irregular.
- Rival IQ 2025: all-industry average ~4 IG posts/week; top-performing brands ~4.7/week; Media is the most active industry.
- Counterweight (STRONG but old): Bakhshi CHI 2014 found over-posting decreases likes-per-post (likes drop 2x faster than comments). Net: frequency grows accounts at the account level; per-post engagement can dilute. NO randomized study found.

## 9. Celebrity / famous-name subjects

- Tellis et al. 2019 (Journal of Marketing, STRONG): celebrities listed among drama elements that arouse the emotions driving video sharing (positive contributor, no isolated effect size published in the summary).
- Chartbeat/Social Media Today post-click data (WEAK-MEDIUM): brand names in headlines slightly REDUCED post-click engagement vs other categories.
- Rathje 2021 (STRONG, adjacent): naming out-group figures (famous politicians) +67% share odds per mention — fame + antagonism is the measured winner in political news.
- Otherwise NO DATA FOUND: no large-N study isolating "famous name vs unknown subject" CTR/engagement in general news. The hook-doctrine rule "never anchor on unknown brands" is indirectly supported (Banerjee: concreteness and social-singular stories help; unknown names are low-concreteness to the reader) but has no direct measured test.

## 10. Niche competitor audit (Aug 27 2026, own scrape — audit_accounts.py)

134 posts scraped anonymously from 14 accounts (12 newest each, pinned posts
excluded from cadence math). Raw data: ig/audit-accounts.json. Likes visible
on ~55% of posts (IG hides counts on the rest anonymously) — medians use
visible only. Content types labeled by one uniform Claude pass. (MEDIUM:
small-N per account, single snapshot, likes ≠ reach.)

**Cadence of the winners (posts/day, pinned-corrected):** @technology ~7.7,
@getintoai ~8.3, @evolving.ai ~5.3, @eluna.ai ~5.4, @chatgptricks ~3.2,
@techskills ~3.5, @futurism ~0.9 (single nightly 21:00 UTC carousel).
The top AI pages run 3-8 carousels/day. Dormant/reel-only pages sit at the
engagement bottom.

**Format (median likes per 1M followers, cross-account):** carousel 4,381
(9 accounts) vs reel 178 (7 accounts). 9 of 11 active accounts are
carousel-first. The two reel-only pages are the two worst normalized
performers (@techcrunch 103/1M, @theaisurfer 929/1M).

**Content type (median likes per 1M followers, all visible posts):**
story 3,788 (n=5) > wow_fact 2,625 (n=15) > recap 2,555 (n=3) >
guide 1,740 (n=18) > news 1,600 (n=21) > prompt_magnet 1,218 (n=20) >
promo 421 (n=4) > meme 89 (n=12).

**Mix signature of the top pages:** none runs a single type. @technology
(12 posts): 4 news + 3 recap + 2 wow + 1 story + 1 guide + 1 prompt-magnet.
@evolving.ai: 4 news + 4 wow + 1 guide + 1 magnet + 2 meme. @techskills:
4 news + 3 wow + 3 story + 2 guide. Guide-first @chatgptricks still mixes
4 news into 12.

**Posting hours (UTC, 134 posts):** peaks 21:00 (17 posts — the biggest),
13:00 (11), 16:00 (11), 01:00 (11); valley at 23:00 (3).

**Prompt magnets ("comment X, we DM you"):** on big mixed pages they hit
above account median (@evolving.ai 24K, @technology 18K) but pages that are
MOSTLY magnets score poorly (@godofprompt 376/1M) — a spice, not a diet.
