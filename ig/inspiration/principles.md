# Virality principles — evidence-backed rulebook

Injected into every writer prompt (write.py, reel.py). Rules ranked by evidence strength:
[STRONG] = large-scale study or official Instagram statement. [MODERATE] = credible industry data. Nothing here is opinion.
Sources: Nature Human Behaviour 2023 (105K headline A/B tests), Upworthy archive (8,977 tests), Berger & Milkman JMR, Science Advances 2024, Facebook IQ, Mosseri official statements (Jan 2025), Meta policy docs, Socialinsider (15-35M posts), Metricool (24.3M posts).

## 1. Hooks & wording

- [STRONG] You get ~1.7 seconds of dwell on mobile. The hook must land with zero context, mid-scroll, half-attention.
- [STRONG] Information gap (Loewenstein): reveal the ACTOR and the EVENT, withhold the CONSEQUENCE. The withheld piece is what forces the swipe. Never resolve the gap in the hook itself.
- [STRONG] Negativity outperforms: each negative word in a headline ≈ +2.3% CTR; positive words reduce clicks. Threat/loss/failure framing beats triumph framing when both are true.
- [STRONG] High-arousal emotion drives sharing: awe, anger, anxiety, amusement. Low-arousal (contentment, mild interest, sadness) suppresses it. "Impressive" is not an emotion — "this shouldn't be possible" is.
- [STRONG] Simple words win: 8th-grade vocabulary beats clever wording. Short common words, short sentences. Never trade clarity for wit.
- [OWNER RULE — hard requirement] Write for a smart 16-year-old. Zero industry jargon: never "frontier models", "agentic", "runtime", "SDK", "inference", "compute", "open-source stack", "infrastructure" — say what the thing DOES in everyday words ("runs powerful AI on your own computer", "a robot that walks like a person"). If a technical term is unavoidable, explain it in the same sentence in plain words. Test every line: would a 16-year-old with no tech background get it instantly? If not, rewrite.
- [OWNER RULE — hard requirement, Jul 28] DASHES ARE BANNED in every published word: no em dash (—), no en dash (–), no " - " used as punctuation — in headlines, sublines, bodies, captions, and reel titles. The em dash is the #1 AI-writing tell. Use a comma, a period, or a colon instead. Hyphens INSIDE compound words are fine (first-sale, AI-written, 5-day).
- [MODERATE] Concreteness is an inverted U: one specific vivid detail beats generic, but piling on details kills the curiosity gap. One number, one name, one twist.
- [MODERATE] Moral-emotional words (banned, exposed, refused, stole, betrayed) add +17-20% each when TRUE.
- [MODERATE] Second-person "you" and direct questions lift engagement (+137-257% in comment studies). Use when the story genuinely touches the reader.
- [OWNER DOCTRINE — Jul 28] WRITE FOR A YOUNG SCROLLER, NOT A NEWSPAPER READER. News-agency register is BANNED: "announced", "reported", "unveiled", "launched an initiative", "users can now", "according to" — that's how Reuters writes for old people, and it is boring. Reference cover (@technology story post): "A GUY MADE AN APP THAT TEXTED 800 GIRLS ON INSTAGRAM WHILE HE SLEPT USING [tool]". The shape: a PERSON ("a guy", "a dad", "a 19-year-old", "a student") + ONE outrageous concrete outcome with a number + an effortless-or-forbidden twist ("while he slept", "in one day", "for free", "and got banned"). Whenever a story CAN be told through the person who did it, tell it through the person — an institution (company, lab, regulator) only stars when the reader's own money/phone/job IS the story.
- [OWNER DOCTRINE — Jul 28] STAKES A 19-YEAR-OLD FEELS: DMs and dating, games, money made while sleeping, their phone, getting banned/caught, side hustles, school, first jobs. Translate every corporate fact into one of these before writing ("cut inference costs 50%" → "AI just got half price for every app on your phone"). If a fact can't be translated into something a young scroller feels, it doesn't belong on a slide.
- [OWNER DOCTRINE — Jul 28] THE MONEY ARC is the single strongest hook shape for this page (reference, @techskills: "A 20-YEAR-OLD CHINESE STUDENT SPENT $20 ON CLAUDE, BUILT AN AI SPEED RADAR IN 9 DAYS, AND SOLD IT FOR $317K"). The arc: SPECIFIC PERSON + TINY SPEND ($20) + BUILT A REAL THING + SHORT TIME (9 days) + BIG MONEY OUT ($317K). Small-in, big-out, fast, by a nobody. One plain sentence a 16-year-old repeats to a friend. When a true story fits this arc, it outranks every other candidate that day. Never fake the numbers; if a link in the arc is missing (no sale price, no timeline), drop that link, keep the rest true.
- [OWNER DOCTRINE — Jul 28] "PEOPLE ARE LETTING AI DO X FOR THEM" is a first-class content type (reference cover: "PEOPLE ARE LETTING CLAUDE TRADE FOR THEM"). Ordinary people quietly handing a real job (trading, coding, selling, customer service) to an AI and getting money/results. Present tense, plural, already happening — the reader feels late. The owner's verdict on this content: "so interesting i would love to read about it. this is the type of content." Scout and writers hunt for these stories every day.
- [OWNER DOCTRINE — Jul 28, second reference batch] SIX MORE PROVEN HOOK SHAPES (real covers the owner flagged as incredible; use whichever the story fits, never let one day's posts all wear the same one):
  - QUIET GOLD RUSH: "PEOPLE ARE QUIETLY PRINTING CASH WITH FREE GITHUB REPOS" — plural ordinary people + a money verb + a FREE or ordinary tool. The payload slides then NAME each real tool one by one (Claude ads, Fincept Terminal...) and what people do with it.
  - FREE GIVEAWAY: "BIG TECH COMPANIES ARE GIVING AI CERTIFICATIONS AWAY FOR FREE" — famous giver + genuinely valuable thing + free. Save-magnet: each payload slide names one item and exactly where to get it.
  - FORBIDDEN KNOWLEDGE: "SECRET CODES FOR CLAUDE THAT BREAK ITS LIMITS" — secret / hidden / breaks-its-limits vocabulary. Every "secret" must be a REAL feature, setting, or prompt that works; forbidden framing on true utility, never clickbait on nothing.
  - EARLY-WINDOW ANALOGY: "LEARNING CLAUDE RIGHT NOW IS LIKE BUYING BITCOIN IN 2016. HERE ARE 7 PROMPTS YOU NEED TO MASTER TO GET RICH IN 2026" — anchor the opportunity to a famous fortune everyone regrets missing (Bitcoin 2016, Amazon 1997), then an N-promise. The payload delivers the actual prompts written out in full, short enough to retype.
  - AI ORACLE: "CHATGPT WAS ASKED WHAT IT WOULD DO AS A HUMAN FOR ONE DAY, AND THE ANSWER SHOCKED EVERYONE" — state the question asked of the AI, withhold the answer entirely. Only when the answer genuinely pays off the swipe.
  - N REAL EXAMPLES: bold trend claim + "HERE ARE 12 REAL-LIFE EXAMPLES" — each payload slide is ONE named example with its own proof visual ("1/ fully autonomous valet parking robot" + the footage/photo of exactly that).
  These join THE MONEY ARC and "PEOPLE ARE LETTING AI DO X FOR THEM" as the owner-validated shapes. Common DNA: real names, real tools, real prompts ON the slide — the post teaches and proves, never just claims.
- [MODERATE — practitioner corpus (charlie947, 350K followers), Jul 28] HOOK ARCHETYPE MENU — when hunting the cover angle, try these six shapes and keep the strongest; they force different doors into the same story: NUMBER-LED (the metric carries it: "40% OF HEADLINES..."), CONTRARIAN (state what everyone believes, then flip it), BEFORE/AFTER (a transformation with a digit), AUTHORITY-STEAL (borrow a world-famous name the reader already trusts/fears), ADMISSION (someone concedes a failure or loss — for a news page: "X ADMITS...", "X QUIETLY DELETED..."), FUTURE-SHOCK ("X is about to change" — only with a concrete date/number behind it). Never let 7 posts in a day all wear the same shape.
- [MODERATE — same corpus] TWO-LINE CONTRAST: the strongest hooks are a pair — an opening claim, then a second line that CONTRADICTS, reframes, or undercuts it. Our cover already has this anatomy (headline + kicker subline): make the subline pull AGAINST the headline, never restate it ("NOTHING HAS FLOWN YET" under a triumphant claim is the pattern).
- [OWNER DOCTRINE — hard requirement, Jul 29, outranks every older cover rule] THE INFORMATION GAP COVER: the cover sells CURIOSITY, it never delivers the article. HARD CAP 9 words, aim 4-8. Name WHO + signal something big happened, withhold WHAT/WHY/HOW MUCH — the facts land on slide 2. Owner-rejected real cover: "VISA JUST LAID OFF 2,600 WORKERS TO INVEST IN A TECHNOLOGY MOST BANKS REFUSE TO TOUCH" (tells the entire story on slide 1, nothing left to swipe for, 17 words render tiny-and-towering). Owner's fix: "VISA JUST BET EVERYTHING" / subline "SWIPE TO SEE WHAT THEY'RE DOING". Then every slide answers one question while creating the next one. Sublines with vague stakes ("THE NEW BET CHANGES EVERYTHING") are banned — command the swipe or name a sharp consequence. Restated verbatim after approving the Visa v2 + Starship v2 reposts (Jul 29): "Your job is NOT to explain. Your job is to make someone incapable of not swiping... Never summarize the story. Never explain everything on slide one... If someone can understand the whole story from the cover, you failed. If the cover could describe 100 different stories, you failed. Every hook should make the reader think 'What happened?' or 'Why?' or 'How?'. The moment those questions disappear, rewrite it." The gap cuts BOTH ways: a cover that tells all fails, and a cover so generic it fits any story fails too — anchor to ONE specific actor/event, withhold the rest.
- [OWNER VERDICT — hard requirement, Jul 27] ONE actor, ONE action per hook. Owner-rejected real cover: "TWO OPENAI AIS JUST ESCAPED THEIR TESTING CAGE AND HACKED ANOTHER COMPANY'S SERVERS TO CHEAT ON THEIR OWN TEST" — three chained actions, insider metaphor ("testing cage"), and it resolves itself so there is nothing left to swipe for. The fix pattern: keep only the single scariest action, move the rest to slide 2, withhold the consequence. If your headline contains "AND" joining two actions, cut everything after it. No metaphors a stranger has to decode.

## 2. Algorithm signals (what Instagram actually ranks on)

- [STRONG] Reels reach for NON-followers ranks on: (1) watch time — the first 3 seconds are a hard gate, (2) likes per reach, (3) sends per reach. Sends (DM shares) are the strongest signal for going beyond followers. Write things people forward.
- [STRONG] Hashtags do NOT increase reach (Mosseri, verbatim). They are topic labels/SEO only. Keywords in the caption and in on-screen text DO feed Instagram search.
- [STRONG] Carousels get second-chance impressions: if a viewer skips, Instagram re-serves the post with SLIDE 2 as the cover. Slide 2 must work as a standalone hook, not a continuation.
- [STRONG] Reaction-bait is penalized by Meta policy: "tag a friend", "comment YES", "double tap" reduce distribution. Utility CTAs are safe and boost saves/sends: "save this", "send this to the person who...".
- [STRONG] Originality policy: accounts that repost 10+ times per 30 days without MATERIAL enhancement are removed from all recommendation surfaces. Credit alone does not protect. Unique text overlay, added commentary/context, voiceover = transformation.
- [MODERATE] Under ~1K followers, reels out-reach carousels ~2.4x (median 134 vs 56 views). Reels are the growth engine at our size; carousels are the depth/ER engine.

## 3. Format mechanics

- [MODERATE] Carousels are the highest-ER format. 10-slide carousels cross 2% ER; engagement dips at slides 2-3 and recovers at 8-10 for those who stay. More value-dense slides = more time spent = more distribution.
- [MODERATE] Mixed image+video carousels are the single highest-ER post type (2.33%).
- [MODERATE] Captions: under 30 words scores highest ER, and only the first 125 characters show before "...more" — the payoff sentence must be the FIRST sentence, never buried.
- [MODERATE] Reels: 15-30s is the completion sweet spot. COMPLETION RATE beats raw watch time — cut the clip before it gets boring, start at the most impressive moment, no build-up.
- [MODERATE — practitioner corpus (charlie947), Jul 28] Reel text/captions: TWO key points maximum in a 15-30s reel, never three — the third one is what kills completion. And NEVER state the conclusion ("this changes everything", "the future is here") — give the facts and let the viewer draw it; a conclusion stated is a swipe-away, a conclusion implied is a comment.

## 4. Inner-slide anatomy — measured from @technology's actual carousels (Jul 2026)

How the reference page writes everything AFTER the cover:

- THE QUESTION CHAIN (the storytelling engine, measured word-for-word from their 4-slide Apple settlement post): the cover plants a question in the reader's head; every next slide answers EXACTLY that question in one big statement (headline) + precise details (body), and the answer plants the next question. Cover "APPLE WILL PAY $250 MILLION... UP TO $95 PER DEVICE" (→ do I get $95?) → "THE $95 NUMBER IS A CEILING. Each approved claim starts at $25... claimants must reside in the United States" (→ does MY phone qualify?) → "ONLY SEVEN IPHONE MODELS QUALIFY. iPhone 15 Pro... bought between June 10, 2024 and March 29, 2025" (→ chain complete) → CTA.
- Slide count follows the chain, not a quota: their Apple story = 4 slides, their Light Flip story = 11 (photo dump), their roundups/listicles = 10-12. A tight 4-slide chain beats a padded 9. NEVER pad.
- STORY SELECTION comes first: they pick news that touches the reader's own life — your money, your phone, your job — or names everyone knows (Apple, Tesla, Marvel, Nolan). A technically-impressive but obscure story loses to a smaller story the reader can feel. [OWNER ADDITION — Jul 28] A PERSON doing something outrageous beats an institutional announcement of similar weight: "a 19-year-old built an app that..." outranks "OpenAI released a feature that..." even when the feature is bigger news. Companies announce; people DO things — pick the doer.
- EVERY inner slide is a standalone mini-story: its own hook headline + its own body. Never a continuation slide, never "part 2".
- Inner headline: 5-9 caps words (shorter than the cover), accent color on the claim nouns, connectives white. It withholds exactly one detail ("...FOR THIS ROLE", "...HIT 503M VIEWS") that the body's FIRST sentence resolves.
- HEADLINE DNA (measured Jul 2026, n=75 of their slides vs 31 of ours — these are the exact gaps that make ours feel weaker):
  - VERBS ARE PHYSICAL, PAST-TENSE ACTIONS: parked, gutted, handed, walked, hired, taught, drained, escaped, ordained. Never corporate/state verbs (integrated, deployed, announced, "is using", "means", "finds"). "GOOGLE SILENTLY PARKED A 4GB AI ON MILLIONS OF LAPTOPS" — parked, not installed.
  - A NUMBER IN THE HEADLINE: 40% of their headlines carry one (ours: 19%). If the story has a true number, the headline uses it ($299, 4GB, 10 SETTINGS, 503M).
  - SCARCITY SUBJECTS: "ONE AI", "A SINGLE BUILDER", "WORLD'S FIRST" — 17% of their headlines (ours: 3%). One small actor doing something outsized is their favorite subject shape.
  - SNEAKY ADVERBS when true: quietly, silently, secretly — instant intrigue.
  - HUMAN-STAKES CLAUSE: the twist compares to human limits ("THAT NEVER SLEEP", "HUMAN GYMNASTS PHYSICALLY CAN'T", "FASTER THAN ANY HUMAN ALIVE").
  - EVERYDAY NOUNS ONLY: they write laptops, battery, pools, refrigerator — things a stranger can picture. Swap insider names for what they are ("Hugging Face" → "another AI company's servers") unless the name is world-famous.
  - BANNED: label headlines ("THE REAL AI JOBS STORY", "WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR BUSINESS") and aphorism/motivational-poster headlines ("INVESTORS PAY FOR EXECUTION, NOT PERFECTION"). Zero instances in their corpus — every slide headline is a standalone factual CLAIM someone could disagree with.
- Body: 2-3 sentences, EVERY sentence carries a concrete number/name/date in bold. Examples measured: "Gabi became the world's first robot monk on May 6, 2026... The 130cm (4.3 ft) Unitree G1 humanoid... received a 108-bead rosary".
- Every fact is framed as a RANK or RECORD when true: FIRST, BIGGEST, EVER, MOST-VIEWED, 2ND BIGGEST TRAILER LAUNCH EVER. Plain facts get upgraded to standings.
- Facts are anchored to famous names the reader already knows: "PIXAR-TIER", "BEATING DEADPOOL & WOLVERINE'S SUPER BOWL SPOT", "NOLAN'S ODYSSEY". Borrowed fame = instant scale.
- A huge stat is sometimes overlaid directly on the photo ("503M") — the number IS the visual.
- IMAGE DOCTRINE (measured): every slide's image is a PROOF ARTIFACT, never decoration — video stills, app/settings screenshots (red arrow on the exact button), tweet screenshots, candid demo photos, claim-form-and-cash style composites. Covers: a human face or a product in action. They essentially never run a text-only or logo-only slide; when no footage exists they use an editorial render. Our logos-on-black fallback is emotionally flat — use it only when every image candidate fails.
- [STRONG] FAMOUS FACES OR NO FACES (owner rule Aug 1, re-verified against the @technology reference set): every image subject is either (a) a RECOGNIZABLE famous person — a CEO of cool tech, a named founder — from a real photo or an explicitly-named likeness, (b) the product/brand itself — hero product shot with the logo or brand mark IN the frame (MacBook under a giant Apple "PRO", PS controller wearing the PlayStation logo), or (c) a real screenshot/photo of the actual thing that happened. NEVER an AI-generated unfamiliar face: a generic invented person nobody recognizes converts badly and has no good outcome. Non-famous people appear faceless (from behind, silhouette, hands only) or not at all — let the product, logo and stakes carry the frame.
- Register: understated authority. Zero exclamation marks, zero emojis on slides, zero hype adjectives (insane/crazy/mind-blowing). The facts carry the drama; hype would cheapen them.
- Single-story posts: slide 2 = a SECOND cover with a different-angle hook + dense all-caps fact block; slide 3 = bullet spec list (bold key terms, price + ship date always last); middle slides = pure photos with NO text (visual breathing room that pads swipes); final slide = CTA selling the page as a news service ("WE SHARE DAILY UPDATES ON WHAT'S HAPPENING IN TECH"), never "follow me".
- Listicle/how-to posts: headline "N) IMPERATIVE VERB + SETTING", one caps sentence on WHY it matters, then "HOW TO:" with numbered steps 1-4, over an annotated screenshot (red arrow on the exact button). This is the save-magnet format.
- The caption is the DEPTH layer: calm 2-3 paragraph prose explainer with the full specs — slides punch, caption explains.

## 5. The business funnel — $100M Offers rules (Hormozi) [OWNER DOCTRINE]

The page exists to get US business owners to book a meeting. Every post serves reach; the pivot slide and caption serve conversion. These rules come from the owner's $100M Offers notes and are mandatory:

- THE PAIN IS THE PITCH. The pivot slide opens with the reader-owner's pain in their own words (hours lost, staff cost, missed calls, unanswered leads) — never with our solution. Name the pain, then show the escape.
- THE VALUE EQUATION drives every conversion line: Value = (dream outcome × belief it will work) ÷ (time to result × effort required). In copy: state the outcome with a NUMBER, prove it with a real case, and always shrink time and effort ("without hiring anyone", "set up once, runs every day", "you don't touch any tech").
- FAST BEATS FREE. Speed-to-result language outperforms savings language: "answers every customer in 30 seconds" beats "saves money on support".
- DONE-FOR-YOU beats do-it-yourself: sell handing the work off, not learning a tool. Business owners don't want AI — they want the job done.
- SPECIFICITY IS PROOF. "$12,000 a year", "14 hours a week", "38 days" — one exact number is more believable than any adjective. Never round a real number into a vague claim.
- NEVER TALK PRICE on the page (owner rule: no pricing shown, USA only). Value only; price lives in the meeting.
- NO FAKE SCARCITY OR URGENCY, ever. Only claims we can actually enforce (a solo consultancy taking limited clients IS honest scarcity). One faked deadline kills all future levers.
- CONVERSION CTA = a DM invitation for business owners, separate from the follow CTA, living in the caption: pain + outcome + tiny ask ("Running a business? DM 'AI' ..."). Utility framing, never bait.
