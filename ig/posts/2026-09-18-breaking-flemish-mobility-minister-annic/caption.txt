Tesla's self-driving mode is officially 9.6x safer than a human driver on normal roads, confirmed by a government minister in parliament 🚗

Everyone assumed self-driving was still the risky experiment. Human drivers were the reliable standard. That changed this week.

Belgium's Flemish Mobility Minister, Annick De Ridder, stood in parliament and put the data on the table: a Tesla driving itself on normal streets is 9.6 times less likely to crash than you are. Not on a test track. On real Belgian public roads, from June through August, across 24.6 million kilometers of actual driving. The experiment already ran, and most drivers on those roads had no idea it was happening.

The part nobody expected: the government used Tesla's own data, collected quietly on public streets while ordinary drivers went about their day, to officially declare in parliament that human drivers are the dangerous party. A minister stood at a podium and told the country, backed by 24 million kilometers of road data gathered without anyone opting in: statistically, you are the risk.

Insurance companies now have government-backed proof. If they use it, you could start paying more just because you chose to drive yourself. The robot didn't just get safer. A government put you in the high-risk category.

Self-driving safety has been debated for years. It just walked into parliament with 24 million kilometers of proof behind it. Every insurance actuary is paying attention now.
