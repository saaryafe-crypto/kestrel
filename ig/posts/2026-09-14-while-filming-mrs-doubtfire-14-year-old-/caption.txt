Robin Williams wrote a personal letter to a school principal begging them to readmit a girl they expelled for filming Mrs. Doubtfire. They framed it. 🎬

Lisa Jakub was 14 when she landed a role in Mrs. Doubtfire alongside Robin Williams. Her Canadian school wasn't impressed. She'd missed too many days, and on-set tutoring didn't count. They expelled her.

Robin Williams wrote directly to the principal, personally asking him to let her back in. One of the most famous actors alive, writing a letter for one kid at one school.

The school said no. Then they framed his letter and hung it in the principal's office. The girl that letter was written for never went back.

Put yourself in that room: Robin Williams' personal appeal on the wall in a frame, and the student he wrote it for permanently kept out. The school said no to Robin Williams and hung his letter like a keepsake. That's not enforcing an attendance policy. That's ego.

Some rules look firm in the moment and absurd forever. Lisa Jakub never returned, and this story is still being retold three decades later while nobody remembers the attendance policy.
