An AI called Claude just found a hidden CRISPR-like enzyme in virus DNA that every human researcher had walked right past. 🧬

Scientists have always assumed real discovery in biology needs a trained human mind and years of lab work. Claude ran 950 copies of itself for 21 hours, scanned raw virus DNA, and flagged a weird repeating pattern buried in the sequence. The kind of thing a researcher skims past and forgets. That throwaway pattern turned out to be a complete enzyme system nobody on earth had ever found.

It sits next to a long chain of repeating DNA with the exact structural signature that made CRISPR famous. Here is the part that should stop you: Anthropic admits they have no idea what this enzyme actually does. The AI found it. Its own creators cannot explain it.

CRISPR started this way. A weird repeat pattern in bacteria, discovered and nearly dismissed, that eventually became the gene-editing tool now used to treat cancer and worth tens of billions. If this new enzyme turns out to matter the same way, the discovery credit belongs to a machine, not a scientist, and that touches every grant, every patent, and every biology career including yours.

950 agents ran simultaneously, found something no human found, and nobody yet knows what they found. That is not a tool. That is a new kind of scientist.

AI is moving from assisting science to doing it. The model most people use to write emails just found something that may end up in a biology textbook.
