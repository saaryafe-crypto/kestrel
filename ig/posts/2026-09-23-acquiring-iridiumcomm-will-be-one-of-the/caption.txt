Rocket Lab is buying Iridium, the satellite network keeping militaries connected when all other signals fail. 🛰️

Every morning this week, Rocket Lab was just a rocket company. A scrappy, impressive one, but still. The whole identity was the rocket.

That changed September 22. Rocket Lab is buying Iridium, the network that soldiers, ships, planes, and emergency responders depend on when every other signal goes dark. One private company controlling the entire chain: the rockets that put satellites in orbit and the communications network those satellites run. If you have ever wondered what that kind of end-to-end control actually looks like, this is it.

Then Peter Beck, the founder who named his company after the rocket, went on record: 'I probably shouldn't have called it Rocket Lab. I should have called it Space Lab. The rocket always steals the show.' He said that on the day he announced he was buying a global satellite network. The day the rocket became the least interesting part of what he built.

No other private company on Earth has ever owned both the vehicles that launch satellites and the communications backbone that militaries and emergency services worldwide depend on. The rocket was never the point. It was just the part that made noise.

The space industry has been consolidating for years, but this is a different kind of move. Not just buying a company. Owning the whole infrastructure end to end, from rocket engine to live signal in the field.
