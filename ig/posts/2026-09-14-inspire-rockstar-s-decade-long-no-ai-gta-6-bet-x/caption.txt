Rockstar built GTA 6 for a decade without AI. 600,000 hand-crafted animations. Journalists flew to Scotland and left calling it unlike anything they'd seen. 🎮

Rockstar started on GTA 6 more than ten years ago. At one point, they had four playable characters planned. They cut it back to two. Not because it was cheaper or easier. Because they were worried players wouldn't feel close to four different people at once. They made the harder call and kept building.

While every other studio was using AI to speed up animation and cut costs, Rockstar went the other way. They hired more than 50 real street artists to paint murals inside the game world. They kept a team living in Miami for a decade, talking to ex-criminals, cops, and club promoters, just to make the city feel real. Then they built 600,000 animations. Every single one made by a human. That is 10 times more than GTA V.

This past July, Rockstar flew journalists to their studio in Scotland. Rob Nelson, who has been at the company for 23 years, picked up a controller and played in free roam for two and a half hours. No rehearsed demo. He told the journalists the game is so large that nobody on the team knows all of it.

The people in that room came out describing a world twice the size of GTA V, a map so detailed it shows what loot is inside each building before you rob it. Zero AI generation. Zero shortcuts. Just ten years of people doing the work, one frame at a time. Send this to someone who has been quietly building something for years and hasn't shown anyone yet.

In an industry where every studio uses AI to cut timelines and costs, Rockstar spent a decade doing the opposite. The journalists who saw it described something nobody had built before. Rockstar even skipped a physical disc release to buy themselves more time to keep refining right up to launch.
