The average American family is roughly $44,000 a year short of being able to buy an average home. 🏠

Most people grew up thinking that if you work hard and save up, buying a house is achievable. That was the whole plan.

The Atlanta Fed just ran the numbers. To afford a median-priced home right now, a household needs to earn $124,674 a year. The average American family falls roughly $44,000 short of that. Not a small stretch. An entire second income short.

Here's the part that should bother you: to afford the average American home, you need to earn well above average. The median person is priced out of the median house. Same word. Completely different number.

The families getting locked out are exactly in the middle. Not the poor, who already knew this. Not the wealthy, who bought years ago. The ones making decent money but not quite enough. They keep renting. Every month the money disappears and they own nothing. They watch the gap between owners and renters grow wider every year. The homeownership path that built the middle class is closing.

Home prices outpaced wages for years, and high interest rates made monthly payments even worse. The math only works now if you earn well above average, and for most families, that gap is only getting worse.
