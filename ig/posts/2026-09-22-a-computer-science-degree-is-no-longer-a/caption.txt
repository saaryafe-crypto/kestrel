The Wall Street Journal just declared a computer science degree is no longer a golden ticket, and AI is exactly why 🎓

For years, the plan was simple: study coding, get a job, be safe forever. Computer science was the one degree people called recession-proof. No matter what happened to the economy, coders would always get hired.

The Wall Street Journal just said that guarantee is gone. CS grads are now hitting a job market that doesn't need as many of them, because AI is doing the entry-level coding work they used to get hired to do.

Here's the part that actually stings. The people getting hit hardest aren't the ones who took a risk. They're the ones who tried to be smart and safe. CS was specifically the "I can't lose" fallback. And the real gut punch: the people who paid six figures to learn how to build AI are now the first ones being pushed out by it. The field that created the disruption is first in line to feel it.

If you're in a CS program right now, or still paying off those loans, you have to ask whether your $100,000 bet on the "safe" career just backfired. AI didn't just take a few jobs. It walked straight into the one career path people chose specifically because it seemed untouchable.

This isn't just a CS story. The skills people chose because they touched technology are now closest to the automation. White-collar safety nets don't hold when AI handles the entry work from day one.
