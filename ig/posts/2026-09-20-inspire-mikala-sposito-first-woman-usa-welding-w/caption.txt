She got $2,100 to learn to weld. Now she's the first woman to represent America at the world championships, and SpaceX is already calling.

Mikala Sposito was a teenager in Dexter, Michigan with one dream: learn to weld and build a life with her hands.

She applied for a work ethic scholarship and asked for $2,700. She got $2,100. They told her to find more money elsewhere. You could read that as a ceiling. She read it as a starting point.

She trained 60 to 80 hours a week for two years. Not talented. Just relentless. Senior year of high school she won first place in Michigan and placed 14th in the country. Freshman year of college she won Michigan again and finished 8th nationally. That earned her a spot at the USA Weld Trials in Huntsville, Alabama, where the top 16 welders in America competed for three spots on Team USA.

Mikala won the gold. She became the first woman in US history to represent America in welding at WorldSkills in Shanghai, the Olympics of skilled trades. She has not graduated yet. SpaceX already offered her a job.

When people call her a natural, she corrects them: "I'm not a natural. I worked and trained 60 to 80 hours a week for two years to get here."

She asked for $2,700. She got $2,100. Send this to someone who says they don't have enough to start.

Most of the world writes off skilled trades as a backup plan. Mikala just beat every welder in America, earned a spot on the world stage, and got a call from SpaceX, all starting with $2,100 and a refusal to quit.
