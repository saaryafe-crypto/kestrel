SpaceX's Starlink satellites dodged other objects 207,152 times in just 6 months, one automated swerve every 75 seconds. 🛰️

The assumption was simple: pack more satellites into orbit and everyone gets faster internet. SpaceX launched thousands of them. No one talked much about what filling the world's most congested highway looks like from the inside.

What 207,152 maneuvers actually means: one automated swerve every 75 seconds, nonstop, all day, every day, for six months straight. The system runs entirely on its own, no human approves a single one. It fires when the chance of a collision hits just 3 in 10 million, which sounds almost impossibly small until you realize how often that threshold gets crossed.

Right now, above your head, one of those satellites is doing that math. It has already run that calculation over a thousand times today alone.

Here is the part nobody is saying out loud: SpaceX, the company that filled low-Earth orbit with more satellites than anyone in history, is now taking a victory lap for the 207,152 times those satellites had to dodge the congestion it created. Every new satellite anyone launches, SpaceX included, makes that number bigger and shrinks the margin. One missed swerve does not just wreck two satellites. It starts a chain reaction that could leave low-Earth orbit unusable for decades, and GPS, weather forecasts, and satellite internet go with it.

The race to fill orbit keeps accelerating, and automated collision systems are the only thing standing between the internet you use every day and a debris field nobody can clean up.
