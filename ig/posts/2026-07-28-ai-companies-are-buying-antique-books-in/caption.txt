Swipe to see how AI companies are destroying rare books with hydraulic cutters to train their AI — and why a judge said it's legal 📚

AI companies are ripping pages out of used books with hydraulic cutters, scanning them with industrial equipment, and throwing away the originals. Anthropic paid $1.5 billion to settle author lawsuits over pirated e-books, so the industry switched to physical copies. A judge ruled it fair use under first-sale doctrine. A book database called ISBNdb now sells AI companies up to one million books per order and keeps every buyer's name secret. Pre-2022 books are prized because they're free of AI-written text that already pollutes the internet.

The companies building AI that writes are now destroying the books humans wrote first. Pre-AI text has become one of tech's most valuable resources — and it's vanishing fast.
