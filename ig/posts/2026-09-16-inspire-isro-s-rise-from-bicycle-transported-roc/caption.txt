India started with no rockets and bicycle parts. It became the first country to land near the moon's south pole. 🚀

In 1963, a small team of scientists worked out of a fishing village on India's southern coast. They had no trucks, no facilities, and almost no money. Rocket parts were transported to the launchpad by bicycle and bullock cart. The nose cone was stored in the local church because there was no other building. That was the start of India's space program.

For the next five decades, India's space agency built everything from scratch. No borrowed technology, no massive budget, no guarantee of success. Just decades of work.

In 2013, India aimed for Mars. The whole mission cost $74 million. NASA launched its own Mars probe the same year for $671 million. India reached Mars on the very first try.

In August 2023, India landed a spacecraft near the moon's south pole. Russia had tried the same thing four days earlier and crashed. No country had ever landed there before. India did it first.

They built it all from a bicycle and a borrowed church. Send this to someone who thinks they do not have enough to start.

When India started, the gap between them and the major space powers looked impossible. They crossed it by not stopping. Every builder working from a small budget or a late start is running the same race.
