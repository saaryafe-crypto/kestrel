Warren Buffett just stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway after 61 years. The company is now worth $1.09 trillion. 🏦

Everyone kind of assumed Buffett would just keep going. He'd been running Berkshire for so long that "Warren Buffett retires" genuinely sounded like a joke. Yesterday, it actually happened. On September 18, 2026, he officially stepped down as chairman after 61 consecutive years at the same company. 61 years is longer than most adults have been alive.

Here's the part that doesn't get talked about enough. He didn't build this from a bank, a tech company, or some big invention. He bought a struggling textile company. A literal fabric mill that was heading toward the scrap heap. Over 61 years, that same company became worth $1.09 trillion, with total growth of over 7,700,000%. That number is real.

Whoever runs Berkshire next has the portfolio but not the reputation he spent six decades building. If you have any savings or money in a retirement account, his way of thinking about which companies to own has quietly shaped how a lot of that gets managed.

7,700,000% in 61 years started with a failing clothing factory, and it ended yesterday.

61 consecutive years at one company is a record no one alive is even chasing. The question isn't just who fills the seat. It's whether anyone can match 7,700,000% in 61 years. No one alive is even close.

Save this. 61 years and 7,700,000% at one company, that's the number.
