SpaceX just flew a rocket nose cone for the 40th time ever, and it used to get thrown in the ocean after every single launch.

Everyone treated rocket nose cones as single-use. After every launch they'd hit the ocean. One flight and they were gone.

One just flew for the 40th time. Same nose cone shell. 40 launches. Still flying. That has never happened to any nose cone in SpaceX history.

Here's the part that gets you: this isn't the big booster. It's not the engines. It's the nose cone, the shell at the tip of the rocket that protects whatever's inside. The exact part everyone assumed was just disposable packaging. Your phone case gets more reuse than this thing used to. Now it's at 40.

Every time that shell flies again instead of sinking, a launch costs less. A Falcon 9 fairing costs roughly $6M new, 40 reuses on one shell means SpaceX avoided buying 39 replacements.

SpaceX started by reusing boosters. Now a nose cone is at 40 flights.
