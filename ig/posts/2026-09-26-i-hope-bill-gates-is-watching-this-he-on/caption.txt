Bill Gates said electric semi-trucks would never happen. Tesla has been shipping them since December 2022.

Bill Gates isn't just a rich guy. He's the person the world paid decades of attention to because he was supposed to be right about where technology was going. On electric semi-trucks, he was not cautious or uncertain. He said it would never happen.

On September 25, a 90-second clip of an electric semi-truck running under a full load went viral on X. By morning, 907,900 people had watched it, with Gates named in the opening line.

That phrase, "never happen," is on the record with his name attached to the wrong side of history. He didn't just express doubt. He made a hard call. And the trucks that have been running since 2022 turned that call into one of the most public misses in recent tech history.

Every diesel trucking company, every fuel supplier, and every job that runs on combustion engines just got the loudest possible signal. Your groceries, your clothes, every package at your door moves on a truck. If electric semis are real at scale, every one of those prices shift. The world's most trusted tech voice called it impossible. The truck didn't check his notes.

Electric semi-trucks were supposed to be the idea that smart money killed. They've been moving freight across America for nearly four years.
