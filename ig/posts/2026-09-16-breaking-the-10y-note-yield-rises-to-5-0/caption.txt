Mortgage rates just hit 7.17%, the highest since July 2007, the year before the housing market collapsed. Homeownership is officially a luxury.

Everyone assumed rates would eventually cool down and give first-time buyers a real shot at getting in. That assumption is officially dead.

The average 30-year mortgage rate hit 7.17% this week. If you try to buy a $400k house right now, you're paying roughly $700 more per month than someone who locked in four years ago. That's $8,400 a year extra, for the exact same house.

Here's what makes this wild: the rate the US government pays to borrow money for 10 years just hit 5.04%, which is the exact level it was at in July 2007, the months right before the housing market collapsed and wiped out millions of people. We're back there now. Except home prices are far higher than they were then. There's no cheap correction waiting on the other side this time.

Renters stay renters longer. First-time buyers get priced out permanently. Anyone on a variable rate is getting hit right now. Pending home sales just fell for the fifth straight month, hitting their lowest reading since records began in 2001. Owning a home used to be the baseline. Now it's the finish line nobody can reach.

Rates this high don't just slow home sales. They lock out an entire generation. Every person priced out today is a renter for years, a buyer never, and part of the reason under-35 homeownership just fell to 37%, its lowest share since 1965.
